Product premise

Khafif is an Arabic-first mobile shopping app designed for low-bandwidth awareness and a focused path from catalog to checkout. It is a portfolio project in development, not a finished commercial product.

The visualizations above were drawn specifically for this case study from the actual screen structure, design tokens, and implementation. They are not presented as captured devices, real inventory, or completed end-to-end evidence.

Designing for changing connectivity

The implementation treats connection quality as a visible product condition rather than a hidden technical detail.

A

Useful cached content comes first

Catalog queries use an offline-first network mode and defined cache windows, allowing previously available content to remain useful while fresh data is unavailable.

B

Offline state is explained in context

An offline banner appears near the shopping experience rather than replacing the entire screen with a generic failure message.

C

Actions retain clear boundaries

Adding a local cart item is separate from claiming a completed remote purchase. Demo catalog records and test payments are explicitly non-commercial.

A compact shopping flow

Catalog

The catalog combines debounced search, category chips, a two-column product grid, offline/cache states, and incremental pagination. Product cards expose a name and understandable price range without overcrowding the browsing view.

Product detail

The product screen pairs imagery with title, description, price, variant chips, quantity controls, and a persistent add-to-cart region. Variant selection and stock state determine whether the action is available.

Cart & checkout

The documented flow moves through cart, shipping, test payment, and confirmation. Arabic and English copy, RTL support, and visible feedback are part of the mobile structure.

A small, intentional system

The design system uses a warm background, deep teal primary actions, amber highlights, and IBM Plex Sans Arabic. Minimum touch-target guidance and short functional motion support a utilitarian shopping experience.

The implementation uses Expo Router, TanStack Query, Medusa v2, PostgreSQL, and Docker. Shared interface components include buttons, text, banners, chips, quantity controls, retry states, and product cards.

Current limits & next steps

Khafif is not a production store. Its catalog is demonstration data and payments are test payments. No real customers, revenue, inventory, or commercial activity is claimed.

The repository’s own status notes do not record full physical-device verification or a complete mobile end-to-end run. This case study therefore makes no new device, test-count, or checkout-validation claim.

Next verification priority

Run the complete mobile flow on physical devices, verify Arabic and English layouts, document offline recovery, and replace these visualizations with genuine captures when the implementation is ready.
